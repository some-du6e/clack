Your workspace, without the browser

Clack is a desktop client for Slack, written in Go. There's no Chromium or Electron inside. One native window draws every pixel on your GPU, and your workspace is on screen in about a second.

Download Clack Try the demo

Free and MIT licensed · Alpha builds for Windows, macOS and Linux

Everything where you left it

Clack copies the 2026 Slack desktop app's layout, so the sidebar, sections, tabs, threads and menus are where you'd look for them. It's an alpha. Where a screen isn't built yet, Clack hides its menu item instead of faking it.

Channels

Channels, with everything in them

Grouped messages, reactions, files and link previews, with canvases, lists, pins and bookmarks in the tabs along the top. A live huddle shows in the channel and in the sidebar, along with the faces of the people in it.

Threads

Threads open next to the conversation

A thread opens in a pane on the right with its own message box, so the channel stays in view. Tick "Also send to" and the reply goes to the channel too. Channel details open in the same pane.

Activity

One list for everything that needs you

Mentions, thread replies, reactions, DMs and invites collect in Activity, with All, DMs, Mentions and Threads tabs and an Unreads filter. Pick an item and its conversation opens beside the list.

Themes

Every Slack theme, light or dark

All 28 of Slack's theme presets, custom colours, and dark mode that can follow your system. The theme you pick is saved to your Slack account, the way Slack saves it.

Plugins

Bring your Taut and Slick plugins

Taut and Slick are client mods for Slack, and Clack runs their plugins without changing them. Each one runs in a sandboxed JavaScript engine, QuickJS compiled to WebAssembly, and Clack turns its React components into native widgets.

Also in Clack

  • Huddles

    Join from the channel, from the sidebar or when someone rings you. Captions and stickers work too.

  • Canvases and lists

    Open them from a channel's tabs, file them in your sections, and save list items for later.

  • Search and Ctrl+K

    Search with Slack's filters and suggestions, or jump to any channel or person with Ctrl+K, ⌘K on a Mac.

  • Notifications

    Desktop notifications on Windows, macOS and Linux. On a Mac you can reply right from the notification.

  • Its own sign-in

    You sign in through your browser, the way the Slack app does, and Clack gets a session of its own. Nothing is copied out of your browser.

  • Updates itself

    On all three systems Clack downloads each new release, checks it against the release's SHA256SUMS and applies it when you restart.

Demo

Try it without an account

Run Clack with demo and it opens Quillfeather Paper Co., a made-up paper company. A dozen coworkers post, reply, react, run polls and start huddles while you look around.

The demo runs its own fake Slack server on your machine. Before it starts, Clack refuses every connection that isn't to your own computer, so it can't reach Slack.

# Linux, from the .deb or .rpm
$ clack demo
# macOS
$ /Applications/Clack.app/Contents/MacOS/clack demo
# Windows, in PowerShell
> & "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Programs\Clack\Clack.exe" demo

How we measured

  1. Startup comes from perf run 44184290543 on commit 1f17ed9, 17 Nov 2026. The release binary ran on a GitHub Actions ubuntu-latest runner under Xvfb with Mesa's llvmpipe software renderer. It talked to fakeslackd, a synthetic workspace the size of the Hack Club Slack, with 150 ms added to every API call and 40 ms to every image, and with six plugins on. Over five starts the first frame came 257 to 319 ms after the process started, and the window settled, meaning nothing changed for 1.5 s, after 769 to 1,090 ms.
  2. Memory comes from the same run's Windows job, on a windows-latest runner with no GPU, so Direct3D 11 ran on Microsoft's software renderer. The fake workspace had 65,846 custom emoji, 32,000 users, 2,006 user groups, 300 channels and 120 DMs. 258 MB is the private working set, Task Manager's Memory column, once a warm start settled. A cold start that loads all 32,000 users measured 325 MB. After scrolling through channels of 6,000 messages it peaked at 818 MB, and ten idle minutes later it was back to 527 MB. On the Linux runner the warm start used 230 MB.
  3. The plugin count comes from the Library's index of {{plugins.generated}}, which lists {{plugins.builds}} builds of {{plugins.count}} plugins.
